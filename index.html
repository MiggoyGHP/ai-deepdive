Section 01

The Great Divide

Where the weights live — and why it's the single most important structural feature of the AI stack.

Closed vs Open-weight — the definitions that matter

Closed-weight

Parameters kept private. Access gated through commercial APIs. Pricing power anchored in capability rent, proprietary RLHF, and enterprise distribution.

OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, xAI.

Open-weight

Parameters publicly released. Anyone can self-host, fine-tune, redistribute. Monetized via adjacent businesses (ads, cloud) — not the model itself.

Meta Llama, Mistral, DeepSeek, Alibaba Qwen, Zhipu, NVIDIA Nemotron.

The nuance: "open weight" ≠ "open source". True open source requires training data + code + weights — which almost nobody ships. Meta's Llama license even caps commercial use above 700M MAU. It's source-available, not open source.
Section 02

Strategic Frameworks

Two playbooks, two different games.

Closed-weight playbook — Capture the Capability Rent

If a model automates a $150,000 engineer's output — as Anthropic's Claude Code increasingly does — then charging $200 / month per seat captures only a fraction of the value created.

The economics: training cost is fixed, revenue scales linearly with enterprise seat count. Software-like growth, infrastructure-like scale.

Open-weight playbook — Commoditize your complements

META's Llama is the canonical example of Joel Spolsky's maxim. Meta's revenue comes from ads, not model licensing. By open-sourcing the frontier, Meta undermines OpenAI and Anthropic — without cannibalizing its own revenue.

Same logic for BABA (Qwen drives Alibaba Cloud), Mistral (drives its inference service), and the Chinese labs (openness sidesteps US chip export controls and builds global developer loyalty).

The risk: free-rider economics. Meta has reportedly invested $65B+ in AI capex in 2026 with no direct licensing revenue. The bet — that AI capability becomes foundational to ads + Reality Labs — is unproven and has generated shareholder friction.

Tier 3 · Direct pre-IPO secondaries

Secondary marketplaces Hiive and Forge facilitate direct purchase of shares from employees, early investors, and VC firms. Highest purity, lowest liquidity, biggest tickets.

Anthropic is among the most active names on Hiive — 46 active orders as of the report's writing. OpenAI, xAI, and Cerebras shares are also directly available.

Practical takeaway: accredited-investor only. $25–250k typical tickets. Spreads matter — reserve for accounts with multi-year lockup tolerance. For sub-$100k the CEFs (Tier 2) are usually more efficient.

The Physical Stack

Picks and shovels — every AI lab buys from the same supply chain.

The AI supply chain — full stack

Both open-weight and closed-weight labs consume the same physical supply chain. The competitive question between model camps is almost entirely upstream of the silicon. All of them land on TSMC-fabricated, SK Hynix-memoried, CoWoS-packaged NVIDIA (or hyperscaler equivalent) GPUs.

Three structural bottlenecks

1. CoWoS

The single most binding constraint. Capacity ~75k wafers/mo in 2025, expanding 30% into 2026 but still sold out through mid-2026.

TSM, ASE, Amkor

2. HBM Memory

SK Hynix ~62% share, supplies ~90% of NVDA's HBM. Entire 2026 capacity already allocated. HBM4 ramp in 2026.

MU · SK Hynix · Samsung

3. High-NA EUV

ASML monopoly — required for Intel 18A and sub-1.4nm nodes. ~$380M per tool vs $180–200M standard EUV.

Synthesis & Risks

Three truths most allocators haven't fully internalized.

Tailwinds · Headwinds · Top Risks

Tailwinds

  • Enterprise adoption from pilots to production; MCP crossed 97M installs in March
  • Capital availability: $188B in Q1 2026 AI mega-deals
  • OpenAI $25B ARR, Anthropic $30B — both growing 200%+ YoY
  • Cost deflation: DeepSeek V3.2 ≈ 90% of GPT-5.4 quality at ~1/50 price
  • IPO pipeline thawing: SpaceX/xAI, OpenAI, Anthropic all telegraphing 2026–27

Headwinds

  • Energy: PJM 6GW reliability shortfall by 2027; 27 US states considering "large load" legislation
  • DC pipeline: only ~1/3 of announced 240GW actually being built; $500B Stargate stalled
  • Regulatory: EU AI Act enforcement, state-level moratoriums, NYT copyright suit
  • Inference commoditization: base-intelligence layer pricing racing to zero
  • Float scarcity: ~$3T combined post-IPO cap with 3–8% floats could cause extreme distortion
Top 5 risks to monitor: (1) DeepSeek V4 launch and pricing · (2) hyperscaler capex cuts · (3) post-IPO float mechanics on ~$3T combined cap · (4) state-level DC moratoriums · (5) PJM 6GW shortfall binding by 2027.

Goldman's suggested core basket (60–70% of AI sleeve): NVDA, MSFT, GOOGL, TSM, ASML, AMZN, META. Satellite: AVGO, MU, AMD, DELL, VST, CEG.
