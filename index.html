Section 01

The Great Divide

Where the weights live — and why it's the single most important structural feature of the AI stack.

Closed vs Open-weight — the definitions that matter

Closed-weight

Parameters kept private. Access gated through commercial APIs. Pricing power anchored in capability rent, proprietary RLHF, and enterprise distribution.

OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, xAI.

Open-weight

Parameters publicly released. Anyone can self-host, fine-tune, redistribute. Monetized via adjacent businesses (ads, cloud) — not the model itself.

Meta Llama, Mistral, DeepSeek, Alibaba Qwen, Zhipu, NVIDIA Nemotron.

The nuance: "open weight" ≠ "open source". True open source requires training data + code + weights — which almost nobody ships. Meta's Llama license even caps commercial use above 700M MAU. It's source-available, not open source.

The convergence curve — why pricing power is a clock, not a moat

Capability gap between closed frontier and open frontier — measured in months, not quality points.

The read: the closed curve flattens (frontier model gains are increasingly marginal); the open curve steepens (Meta's capex, Chinese labs, NVDA's own open models compound). Pricing power is a function of how long the gap stays open, not how wide it is. Every month the gap narrows, closed-lab ARPU faces compression.
Section 03

Compute Regimes

Two compute markets, not one. Training vs. inference. Different chips win each.

Training vs Inference — the inversion that already happened

v1 treated AI compute as one market. v2 explicitly splits it. In 2025, inference compute spend industry-wide overtook training — and is now growing at ~2× the pace. Different workloads reward different silicon.

Training — the spectacle

  • One-time, batch, throughput-bound
  • 50,000–200,000 GPU-equivalents synchronous, 60–180 days
  • $1–5B per frontier run at 2026 prices
  • HBM-bandwidth bound (4–8 TB/s on B200/B300)
  • All-to-all communication → NVLink/NVSwitch fabric is decisive
  • Hardware default: NVDA H200/B200/B300/GB300 NVL72

Inference — the recurring revenue

  • Online, latency-bound, batched, recurring
  • KV-cache memory pressure (long-context = 100s GB/req)
  • Cost scales linearly with usage
  • Reasoning-mode multiplies per-query compute 25–250×
  • Heterogeneous chip mix wins (B200 prefill + L40s/MI300X decode)
  • Custom ASIC fits well: TPU v6e, AWS Inferentia, Meta MTIA
Trader read: the inference shift re-rates the supply chain toward memory-bandwidth-rich and capacity-rich chips — favoring HBM4 (SK Hynix, MU) and custom ASICs (AVGO, MRVL) over peak-FLOPs training silicon. v2 explicitly calls AVGO "the most undervalued name in the entire AI silicon complex."

Trader takeaway: NVDA's growth story has shifted from "capture all of AI compute" to "remain dominant at training while ceding select inference share to AVGO/MRVL custom silicon." Not bearish for NVDA — but supports a re-rating thesis for AVGO that v1 understated.

The bottleneck funnel — $500B of demand, one choke point

Pick any AI lab — open or closed, US or China. The physical path is identical. The trade isn't the model; it's the pinch.

INPUTS
~$500B/yr hyperscaler capex · Raw silicon wafers · 325–580 TWh US datacenter demand by 2028 · HBM DRAM supply
CHOKE POINTS
~75k wafers/mo — sold out through mid-2026
62% pre-allocated to NVDA; '26 fully booked
~$380M/tool · handful of tools shipped
OUTPUTS
NVDA Blackwell / Vera Rubin GPUs · AMD MI-series · Custom ASICs (TPU, Trainium, MTIA) · Deployed datacenter capacity
The investment logic: demand-side uncertainty is enormous (which lab wins? which model? open or closed?). Supply-side uncertainty is tiny — everybody routes through the same three names. That's why the picks-and-shovels trade (TSM · ASML · MU) carries less thesis risk than owning any single model lab.

Apple — the local-AI hardware fork (NEW v2 — Tactical Overweight)

v1 missed Apple entirely. v2 calls it the quiet hardware winner of the local-AI thesis — not a model winner, but the de facto reference platform for at-home/small-business AI inference.

Why Apple silicon won locally

On a discrete-GPU PC, GPU is bandwidth-isolated from system RAM, capped at 16–80GB VRAM. Loading a 70B-param open-weight model on a single consumer GPU is essentially impossible. By contrast:

  • M3 Ultra Mac Studio: up to 512GB unified memory @ ~800 GB/s for $9,500
  • Quantized Llama 4 Maverick / DeepSeek V3.2 / Qwen 3.5 Max serve from a single desktop
  • Multi-GPU server build for equivalent scale: 5–10× the cost
  • MLX framework: 25k+ GitHub stars, default backend for Ollama on macOS + LM Studio
  • Mac Studio ≥256GB RAM: 2–4 month back-order on Apple's online store as of April 2026

v2 catalyst path for AAPL

  • Jun 2026 — WWDC: Apple Intelligence v3, on-device reasoning, MLX v2
  • Rumored: M-series Pro datacenter chip for Private Cloud Compute, possibly third-party available
  • Services uplift: Apple Intelligence Plus subscriptions if launched
  • Apple Intelligence partners: MSFT/OpenAI (US), BIDU (China)
Trader takeaway: AAPL's AI thesis is unconventional — it's a hardware thesis on a non-model leader. v1 didn't capture this vector at all. Read-through trade: long TSM as Apple is its largest customer; MLX framework expansion = wafer demand.

The Agentic Runtime Layer (NEW v2 — value-capture above the model)

A new value-capture stratum that didn't exist as an investable category in 2024. Sits between the model and the end user — orchestrates model calls, tool invocations, persistent memory. Closer to an OS abstraction than a chat UI.

Open standards / runtimes

  • MCP (Model Context Protocol) — Anthropic → Linux Foundation; 97M installs Mar 2026
  • AGENTS.md — OpenAI → Linux Foundation; markdown agent capability/policy declaration
  • OpenClaw — open-source, foundation-stewarded; 250k+ GitHub stars; runs Claude/GPT/Kimi/GLM/MiniMax interchangeably; spans US-China divide (Tencent + Z.ai adoption)
  • goose — Block (formerly Square); donated to Linux Foundation Agentic AI Foundation
  • Cline / Roo — independent open-source coding agents in VS Code

Proprietary runtimes

  • Cursor (Anysphere) — AI-native IDE w/ Composer 2; reportedly $200M+ ARR; private
  • Claude Code / Cowork — Anthropic's first-party; primary driver of Anthropic's enterprise mix
  • Codex / GPT Apps — OpenAI's first-party via AGENTS.md
  • Bolt / Lovable — StackBlitz / Lovable AB; AI-native website + app builders for non-devs; venture-stage
Trader read: the runtime captures behavioral data + persistent context — the new locus of customer relationship. The model below is closer to an interchangeable supplier. v2's strategic call: closed labs own first-party productivity (Claude Code, ChatGPT Pro Agents); independent runtimes (Cursor, OpenClaw) dominate developer/prosumer by being model-agnostic and faster-iterating.

Section 07

Synthesis & Risks

Three truths most allocators haven't fully internalized.

Tailwinds · Headwinds — by camp (v2 §9.3 + §9.4)

Open-camp tailwinds

  • Inference cost arbitrage as enterprise inference volume scales
  • Sovereign / regulatory mandates (EU, India, ME, ASEAN) favor self-hostable stacks
  • Architectural commoditization (sparse MoE, reasoning) erodes closed-weight technical moat
  • Chinese cohort proves frontier capability at 1–2 OOM smaller training budgets

Closed-camp tailwinds

  • Enterprise mix scaling faster than consumer (higher-margin, stickier)
  • Agentic reliability moat — hardest to replicate at frontier parity
  • Ecosystem lock-in via Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, GPT Apps compounding
  • Regulated industries (defense, healthcare, finance) closed-weight by mandate

Open-camp headwinds

  • Monetization structurally weak — Meta no direct rev; CN labs subsidized by parent clouds
  • Safety / red-team / eval infra weaker → higher enterprise TCO
  • Talent retention hard when lab doesn't capture upside (DeepSeek, MiniMax, Mistral departures)

Closed-camp headwinds

  • Per-query unit economics deteriorating as reasoning compute outpaces price resets
  • Defending the frontier costs 3–5× per generation if capability gains slow
  • Open-weight floor compresses addressable price ceiling over time
  • Hyperscaler partner-compete (MSFT MAI-1, AMZN Nova, GOOGL prioritizing Gemini)
Top 5 systemic risks (v2): (1) Capability plateau at frontier compresses closed pricing premium · (2) Hyperscaler capex pause (AI rev disappointment OR PJM grid binding) · (3) Geopolitical de-escalation reverses parallel-stack capex · (4) NVDA customer concentration (SK Hynix 90% to NVDA; CRWV >50% MSFT) · (5) Per-token pricing premium 30× → ~3–5× by 2027.
